# Introduction to Networks — Key Points

## What It Is

- A network is a collection of interconnected devices ("nodes") that can communicate — sending/receiving data and sharing resources.
- Nodes include computers, smartphones, printers, and servers.
- Key concepts: Nodes (individual connected devices), Links (wired or wireless communication pathways connecting nodes), and Data Sharing (the core purpose of any network).

## Room Analogy (Core Concept)

- A group of friends chatting in a room: each person is a device (node).
- Their ability to talk and listen represents the communication links.
- The conversation itself is the data being shared.

## Why Networks Are Important

- Resource Sharing – multiple devices can share hardware (e.g., printers) and software.
- Communication – instant messaging, emails, and video calls all rely on networks.
- Data Access – files and databases can be reached from any connected device.
- Collaboration – enables real-time teamwork even across long distances.

## Types of Networks

**Local Area Network (LAN)**
- Covers a small geographical area (home, school, small office).
- Typically owned/managed by a single person or organization.
- High data transfer speed.
- Uses wired (Ethernet) or wireless (Wi-Fi) connections.

**Wide Area Network (WAN)**
- Spans a large geographical area (cities, countries, continents), connecting multiple LANs.
- Often has collective/distributed ownership (e.g., ISPs).
- Slower speeds than LANs due to long-distance data travel.
- Uses fiber optics, satellite links, and leased telecom lines.
- The Internet is the largest example of a WAN.

**LAN vs. WAN Comparison**
- Size: LAN is small/localized; WAN is large/broad.
- Ownership: LAN is single-entity; WAN spans multiple organizations/providers.
- Speed: LAN is high; WAN is comparatively lower.
- Maintenance: LAN is easier/cheaper; WAN is more complex/costly.
- Example: LAN = home/office network; WAN = the Internet.

## How LANs and WANs Work Together

- A home LAN connects to an ISP's WAN to gain Internet access for all devices in the home.
- The modem acts as a bridge, converting digital signals from the router into a format suitable for transmission over telephone lines, cable, or fiber optics.
- In businesses, multiple office LANs are linked via WANs for unified communication and collaboration across locations, enabling shared databases and real-time teamwork.
- Example scenario: home devices connect to a router (forming a LAN); the router communicates with the ISP's WAN, giving the home network access to websites and online services worldwide.

## Why It Matters

- Understanding LANs and WANs is foundational knowledge for entering cybersecurity.
- The growing interconnectedness of devices worldwide drives increasing demand for security professionals.

## Q&A – Questions and Answers

**Q: What is the term for a collection of interconnected devices that can communicate and share resources with each other?**

**A**: Network

**Q: In network terminology, what is the term for individual devices connected to a network?**

**A**: Nodes

**Q: What is the largest Wide Area Network (WAN) that connects millions of Local Area Networks (LANs) globally?**

**A**: Internet

**Q: What is the acronym for a network that connects devices over a short distance, such as within a home, school, or small office building?**

**A**: LAN

**Q: In networking, what term describes the communication pathways (wired or wireless) that connect nodes?**

**A**: Links
